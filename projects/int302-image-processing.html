INT302 · Image Processing · Individual laboratory work

From pixels to face representations

01 · The question

How do local pixel operations change visual quality, and how can a compact linear representation support face matching?

02 · My implementation

Implemented neighborhood analysis, resampling and filtering, followed by eigenface projection, reconstruction and distance-based matching in two individual MATLAB labs.

03 · The evaluation

Compared boundary choices, noise and interpolation behaviour; examined orthogonality, representation quality and sensitivity to luminance and noise.

04 · The conclusion

Representation and numerical assumptions matter. The reports provide the submitted results; the browser example explains interpolation without claiming a fresh MATLAB run.

Two MATLAB labs exploring how local pixel operations improve images, and how eigenface projections turn face images into compact feature vectors.

Lab 1 · Image fundamentals

I implemented 4- and 8-neighborhood extraction, boundary handling, local averaging, nearest-neighbor and bilinear resampling, noise generation and filtering experiments.

The report compares visual quality with MSE and PSNR, and discusses the trade-off between smoothing noise and preserving detail.

Lab 2 · Eigenfaces & enhancement

I checked a supplied eigenface basis, projected images into that basis, reconstructed images from weights and implemented distance-based identity matching.

The report investigates noise and luminance sensitivity and manually implements histogram equalization.

What this taught me

Image quality depends on boundary conditions, intensity scale and the choice of representation. Eigenfaces connect linear algebra with recognition, while the noise experiments show why a compact representation needs robustness checks.

Evidence and reproducibility

The linked reports document the submitted experiments. Source downloads contain the original MATLAB files; third-party test images and course datasets are excluded. Lab 1 uses Image Processing Toolbox. Lab 2 references data_for_eignfaces.mat, which was not included in the supplied archive.

The MATLAB experiments have not been rerun here. The PSNR helper uses a peak of 255 while the Lab 1 report describes normalized intensities; that scale must be checked before reusing reported scores. The Lab 2 matcher uses eight eigenfaces and adds random noise to the input weights, so its results should not be presented as a deterministic production face-recognition system.
