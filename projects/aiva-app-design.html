01 · Problem & goal

Problem & goal

Make planning, nutrition and everyday decisions easier through a coherent personal-assistant interface.

What the requirements changed

The presentation reports 70 valid survey responses and identifies schedule planning, health management and decision support as recurring needs. The design brings these functions into one assistant rather than treating them as unrelated tools.

  1. Understand routines and needs
  2. Set the assistant’s style and preferences
  3. Choose planning, nutrition or decision support
  4. Receive a concrete next action

03 · Implementation approach

Implementation approach

Start from everyday planning and decision needs, organize the proposed features, then develop interface prototypes and explain the intended interactions in a design presentation. The group concept and individual design development are identified separately.

Concrete design changes documented in the individual presentation

04 · Results & reflection

Results & reflection

The individual presentation documents six stages of interface revision. The changes make the assistant easier to enter, personalize and use, while removing functions that duplicate the calendar.

What is better in the revised design

Design outcomes visible in the presentation
AreaEarlier design issueRevised direction
OnboardingThe assistant’s identity was not clear at entryButler-led login and entry screens; fewer repeated actions
PersonalizationAvatar and wake-word settings were mixed togetherSeparate style selection and customization steps

Evidence & limits

These are interface and interaction-design outcomes. The strongest evidence is the prototype and the documented revision process; launch-user counts and health outcomes are not used here as validated product results.
