Four-in-One Smart Umbrella

A connected umbrella prototype developed first for engineering coursework, then refined into a family-focused competition concept.

01 · Problem & goal

Problem & goal

Help users locate a misplaced umbrella, detect unwanted movement, make returning it easier and improve visibility after dark.

02 · My contribution

My contribution

Hardware selection and procurement, NFC editing tests, lighting-layout iterations, enclosure confirmation, video editing and report writing are documented as my contributions.

03 · Implementation approach

Implementation approach

Stage 1 — English MEC202 coursework: compare tracking options and integrate GPS, 4G, movement sensing, NFC, a buzzer and illumination into a physical umbrella. Stage 2 — later Chinese competition materials: refine the family-use concept around remote location, anti-theft alerts, easier return and visibility. My documented work includes hardware selection, NFC tests, lighting iterations and enclosure confirmation.

Why the team changed the tracking approach

Engineering decisions documented in the English coursework report
Option exploredConstraint encounteredDesign consequence
Bluetooth + GPSShort-range phone connectivity limits recovery after separationMove away from a proximity-only tracking link
Wi-Fi + GPSDependence on reachable networks and unstable connectivitySeek a communication path that works beyond known Wi-Fi
GPS + Air780EX 4GCellular communication can upload location to a serverUse the long-range link, with separate serial handling for GPS and 4G

Source: MEC202 report, GPS and communication-module development.

04 · Results & reflection

Results & reflection

The coursework documents concrete integration fixes; the later competition materials refine the same prototype around four family-use functions. The progression is from module feasibility and testing to a clearer use-case presentation.

Problems encountered and the changes made

Coursework prototype: problem → change → purpose
Observed issueChange described in the reportWhy it matters
Weak GPS reception and failure to obtain a fixReplace the ceramic antenna with an active antennaImprove reception and satellite acquisition
GPS/4G serial conflicts and lost dataSeparate communication using an expansion board and software serialReduce contention between location and transmission modules
Movement alerts triggered by unstable readingsUse a configurable acceleration threshold and button debouncingDistinguish intended monitoring from minor disturbances
Lighting and enclosure attachment needed iterationRefine the illuminated edge and 3D-printed mounting arrangementMake the electronics physically usable on an umbrella
A finder needs a simple way to return the umbrellaWrite owner information into an editable NFC tagEnable return without requiring the finder to use the tracking system

Source: MEC202 report, module implementation, testing and team contributions.

What the later competition materials add

The Chinese competition document frames the work as a family companion: locate, protect, return and remain visible. These are a refinement of the project’s purpose and presentation, not a claim that all four hardware functions were invented only after the coursework.

The course report also identifies an exposed alarm-disable button and incomplete remote control as remaining issues. Those observations motivate the next engineering steps.

Source: Later Chinese competition proposal.

Evidence & limits

The coursework and later competition materials document successive team-development stages. They do not independently establish tracking accuracy, waterproof certification, production readiness or competition awards.
