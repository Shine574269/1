Four-in-One Smart Umbrella

A connected umbrella prototype developed first for engineering coursework, then refined into a family-focused competition concept.

01 · The need

Help users locate a misplaced umbrella, detect unwanted movement, make returning it easier and improve visibility after dark.

02 · My role

Hardware selection and procurement, NFC editing tests, lighting-layout iterations, enclosure confirmation, video editing and report writing are documented as my contributions.

03 · The development

The team compared tracking options, integrated sensors and communication modules, and refined the physical assembly. Later competition materials organize the design around four everyday functions.

04 · The evidence

The English coursework report documents engineering development. The later Chinese competition document and demonstration present the refined entry. They are successive stages of one team project.

Stage 1 · MEC202 coursework · English materials

Build and integrate the prototype

The coursework report compares Bluetooth + GPS, Wi-Fi + GPS and 4G + GPS. The team selected a long-range location link using GT-U8 GPS and an Air780EX 4G module, alongside an MPU6050 movement sensor, NFC tag and buzzer.

Physical iteration mattered: the report discusses a straight-shaft umbrella, the illuminated edge and a 3D-printed electronics enclosure. The project connects requirements and component choices to assembly and testing.

Stage 2 · Later competition development · Chinese materials

Refine the design for family use

The entry for the 8th University Intelligent Robot Creative Competition presents four connected functions: remote location, movement-triggered anti-theft alerts, NFC-assisted return and illuminated visibility.

Find & protect

GPS/4G provides the proposed remote-location link; motion sensing and a local alarm support anti-theft use.

Return & be seen

NFC gives a finder access to return information. Illuminated trim makes the umbrella’s outline visible and supports warning-light modes described in the proposal.

What I learned

A connected product depends on mechanical layout and usable interaction as much as on sensors. NFC return information, lighting attachment and enclosure choices turned an electronics concept into a more practical prototype.

The documents and demonstrations show the team’s development work. They do not establish production readiness, independently measured tracking accuracy, waterproof certification or competition awards.
